# Time and simultaneous actions

## Scope

Session 02 Review uses `time-practice.js`. Boss 01-05 now has a separate
[cross-unit pilot](BOSS_PILOT_DESIGN.md). Both bypass the old stem quizzes, generic transformations, correction
packs, and reading packs. Adaptive review remains disabled. Recent-family history
only helps avoid repeated situations; it does not inject easier remediation.

## Four layers

1. **Lexicon:** the existing shared noun and verb pools, plus explicitly translated
   Time nouns. Verb forms come from the verb pool, not suffix guesses. A bounded
   reading task uses a page/article rather than implausibly finishing a textbook.
2. **Compatibility:** reading/writing/cooking/watching/eating/listening/drinking
   frames restrict objects. Explicit attention-resource edges allow listening
   while reading/writing/cooking and drinking while reading/watching. Meal times
   and film durations have constraints. More edges should be reviewed, not enabled
   by a broad tag alone.
3. **Facts:** named actors, activity intervals, action identity, start/end/current
   times, completion deadlines, and required waiting endpoints.
4. **Assessment:** twelve task families, split fields, explicit accepted variants,
   and a session selector. A 25-item round contains eight simultaneous-action,
   eight action-stage, and nine duration/deadline questions.

## What the questions test

- Activity reports: simultaneous versus sequential, including different actors.
- Combining two actions while preserving an explicitly requested main focus.
- Matching a sentence to actors and overlapping activity records.
- Selecting the main-clause focus without calling the reversed order ungrammatical.
- Choosing, producing, and correcting an action-stage report.
- Producing updates at two independently labelled moments, in either order.
- Two independent time-marker blanks, including both markers being the same.
- Selecting a plan that respects both a completion deadline and a waiting endpoint.
- Changing a promise from continuation to completion, or vice versa.
- Interpreting what a time-bound promise does and does not imply.

Multiple-choice alternatives concern the same actions/times. Stage choices use
three genuine grammatical forms rather than adding an unrelated fourth option.
For writing, verb forms are supplied without the assembled target sentence.
There are no answer placeholders or vocabulary-only answer fields.

## Grammar and grading

- Simultaneous-action ながら uses a stem and shares the actor with the main clause.
  Reversing clauses can be grammatical; focus is graded only when explicitly asked.
- Dictionary / ている / た + ところ distinguish imminent, ongoing, and immediately
  completed actions. The action must remain identical through all stages. These
  tasks avoid result-state ambiguities such as 入る versus finishing a bath.
- まで is an endpoint for continuation. までに is a latest completion time, including
  earlier or exactly-on-time completion. Long activities can take までに too.
- Grading accepts kana, mixed script, punctuation differences, relevant plain/polite
  variants and explicitly enumerated word orders. It does not remove particles,
  negate meaning, substitute actions, or fuzzily ignore a different time.
- Separate fields retain independent feedback. Retries use a fresh situation from
  the same task family and shuffle choices; they never fall back to legacy packs.

The selector caps task counts, rotates topics and action domains, tracks noun use,
and excludes repeated situation families within a round. A different clock or
waiting place alone does not make the same limit task a new family. Choices are
position-balanced within their option count, with no three-answer position streak.

## Sources and limitations

Grammar distinctions were checked against the University of Texas JOSHU notes:
[action stages](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/japanese/joshu/grammar/glist/y2/ch2/gl_y2_ch2_irutokoro.php)
and [until versus by](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/japanese/joshu/grammar/glist/y2/ch1/gl_y2_ch1_made&madeni.php).
For simultaneous actions, see [Tae Kim's verb-tense and clause guide](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/category/complete-guide/verb-tenses-and-clauses/).
Questions are original, not copied exam questions. This is a tested first version,
not native-teacher certification or a measured claim of N4 exam difficulty.

Tests cover independent fact evaluation, answer variants and counterexamples,
multiple seeds/session sizes, semantic timing constraints, legacy-bank isolation,
full UI completion, retry/partial feedback, and desktop/mobile layouts. They do
not prove every possible natural paraphrase will be accepted. Extend the explicit
answer variants when a valid paraphrase is found; do not loosen semantic checks.
